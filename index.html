About

I am a theoretical physicist working on the statistical physics of disordered systems. I study how systems of many interacting units (spins, algorithms, epidemics, opinions, species) evolve in time when they live on sparse random graphs, and I develop local, cavity-based approximations that make this dynamics tractable.

I am an Assistant Professor at the Faculty of Physics of the University of Havana, where I work in the Center for Complex Systems. I received my PhD from the University of Havana in 2024 under the supervision of Dr. Roberto Mulet. My thesis was awarded the prize for the best doctoral thesis in Natural Sciences in Cuba. From 2024 to 2026 I was a postdoctoral researcher at Sapienza University of Rome, in the Statistical Mechanics and Complexity group.

Research

Dynamics on sparse random graphs

Cavity master equations, local closures and dynamic cavity methods for continuous-time stochastic dynamics of discrete variables, including Ising models, epidemics and glassy relaxation.

Algorithms for hard optimization

Local equations that describe stochastic local-search algorithms in random K-SAT, algorithmic thresholds and their time scaling, belief propagation, and benchmarks for graph neural networks.

Theoretical ecology

The generalized Lotka–Volterra model on sparse, asymmetric interaction networks, using Local Fokker–Planck equations to infer species interactions from data.

Social and biological networks

Opinion dynamics and consensus formation under heterophobic interactions, and message-passing models of neural activity in C. elegans.

Projects

CNRS International Research Project · 2027–2029 · Co-leader

RISE: Reconstructing Interactions in Sparse Ecosystems

This is a collaboration between the LPTM at CY Cergy Paris Université (project leader Dr. Matteo Negri), the Department of Mathematical Sciences at the University of Bath (Dr. Pietro Valigi) and the Faculty of Physics of the University of Havana. We adapt Local Fokker–Planck equations, pseudo-likelihood learning and graph neural networks to infer sparse, asymmetric interactions in ecosystems, both from synthetic data and from experimental data. The project funds researcher and student mobility, and a postgraduate student from Havana will be co-supervised in Havana and Paris.

PRIN 2022 PNRR (Italy) · 2024–2026 · Postdoctoral researcher

When deep learning is not enough

The project's full title is "When deep learning is not enough: creating hard benchmarks and physics-inspired algorithms for combinatorial optimization problems." It was hosted at Sapienza University of Rome.
